# Evidence and sources behind the submission

## Primary evidence: 3 doctor conversations (early September 2026)

Reconstructed working notes, not verbatim transcripts or recordings — written
up afterward by Abhijay and Bhaskar from memory of the conversations.

**Who was spoken to:**
- 1 doctor in a government hospital setting (GMCH-32, Chandigarh)
- 2 doctors in private practice

**What came up, unprompted, in each conversation:**
- **Documentation-technicality denials.** Insurers frequently demand a
  specific diagnostic (e.g. an X-ray) as a condition of settling a claim,
  even when the treating doctor's own clinical evaluation didn't require it
  for that case. The claim stalls on a document the treatment never needed.
- **"Discount = fraud" flag.** When a patient receives a surgery at a
  discounted rate, insurers have treated the discount itself as suspicious —
  read as evidence of double-billing or fraud — rather than a legitimate
  negotiated/subsidized cost, delaying or denying the claim.
- **Pre-approval timing** (from Bhaskar's friend's MRI claim). A clinically
  warranted MRI was denied reimbursement because the insurer's
  prior-authorization step wasn't cleared before the scan — not because the
  procedure was invalid, but because of when in the process the paperwork
  happened.

**Why this matters for the product:** all three patterns are the same shape —
the claim is substantively valid but fails on a procedural technicality that
occurs *before* the insurer's own settlement clock starts. This is the basis
for the submitted Q2 insight ("claimants lose before the clock starts") and
for triggering the advance on the unfiled/procedurally-stuck bill rather than
waiting for a formally accepted claim.

## Supporting external data (cited, not primary research)

IRDAI-reported figures, via Business Standard / Business Today, citing IRDAI's
FY24 annual report:
- Insurers denied ~11% of health insurance claims and had ~6% pending as of
  March 2024.
- Disallowed + repudiated claims totaled ~₹26,000 crore in FY24, up 19.10%
  from FY23.
- Sources: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/health-insurers-reject-claims-worth-rs-15-100-crore-in-fy24-irdai-124123100615_1.html), [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/health-insurance-claim-rejections-by-insurance-companies-went-up-by-1910-in-fy24-458593-2024-12-26)

These are market-level context for why the pattern described is plausible at
scale — explicitly not a substitute for, and not presented as, the team's own
primary research.

## The IRDAI settlement-speed number the submission had to pre-empt

IRDAI's own published FY2024-25 data shows **~99.93% of health claims settled
within three months** — a number that, taken at face value, contradicts the
submission's premise that settlement speed is a real pain. The submitted Q2
answer pre-empts this directly by reframing what the clock measures: it
argues the clock only starts at *complete filing*, and the resubmission loops
described by the doctors (unstamped bill, missing summary) happen *before*
that clock starts — so they never show up in the 99.93% figure. This
reframe is the load-bearing move in the submission's insight and should not
be contradicted or walked back in the next round without a deliberate,
flagged decision.

Two other reasons the aggregate stat may understate the pain (considered but
not all written into the final submission — available if a judge pushes on
this in the next round):
- "Settled" in IRDAI's data typically includes fast *rejections*, not just
  paid claims — a quick denial counts as "settled."
- The aggregate blends cashless claims (fast, insurer-controlled) with
  reimbursement claims (slow, paperwork-heavy) — the product targets the
  latter, which the average hides.
